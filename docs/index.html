The Python tools in this repository were rebuilt and this browser port has not been updated. It still taxes ~57 tax-exempt items that owe nothing, assumes every flip occupies a slot for exactly four hours, ignores the high-alchemy floor, and applies no correction for the fact that ranking hundreds of noisy estimates surfaces the largest errors rather than the largest values. Its numbers will disagree with cli.py and app.py, and those are the ones to trust.

Live flip finder for Old School RuneScape, built on the OSRS Wiki real-time prices API. It ranks by expected gp per offer slot per hour, not by the margin on the screen — because a quoted margin is only profit if both legs of the flip actually fill.

How the ranking thinks — four reasons a quoted margin isn't profit

1. You have to get to the front of the queue

The Grand Exchange matches offers on price first, then on age: at the same price, an offer placed days ago has near-absolute priority over yours. So there are two ways to get filled — outbid the queue, or wait in it.

That is why air runes are a trap. Quoted at 5 buy / 6 sell, the 1 gp margin looks like a 20% return on 50,000 units. But to jump the queue you would have to buy at 6 and sell at 5, which loses money. You have zero room to compete, so you are stuck behind thousands of offers and bots for a margin of one coin. Every item gets an undercut room number: how much price improvement it can afford per side. Zero means you cannot influence whether you fill, and the ranking discounts it to 15% of its quoted profit. By default those flips are filtered out entirely.

2. Your offer fills when you least want it to

A resting buy offer fills fastest exactly when the price is falling — someone is dumping into it — and then your sell leg is stranded above the market. Passive orders are selected against. The tool compares the 5-minute and 1-hour averages to detect drift, and penalises a falling market harder than a rising one, because you are the one holding inventory between the two legs.

3. Slots are scarcer than gold

Free-to-play has three GE slots, members eight. You cannot run fifty flips, so "profit if I put my whole bank into this one item" is the wrong question. Your budget is split across your slots, and everything is ranked by what a slot earns per hour.

4. The last print is not the market — and neither is a spike

A few hundred salmon dumped at 30 gp in the last minutes reads as "buy at 30" to every intraday number — while two weeks of sellers accepted 40. A big offer at 30 fills against that one dumper, then sits. And the mirror image is the manipulation trap: an item pumped above its normal level shows a juicy margin exactly while a merch clan is waiting to dump on whoever buys it.

So the top candidates are re-checked against 14 days of history, on market-making terms: what share of real volume traded at your prices, how far today's quote sits above the item's 14-day median (a spike above it is discounted hard — supply and demand for game commodities barely move, so prices revert), and how calmly the price sits in its band. A rise earns no bonus at all: from price data a genuine update-driven demand shift and a pump are identical, so the tool waits for the new level to settle instead of chasing it. Stable staples inside a settled range — the flipper's bread and butter — is what floats to the top.

Intraday prices are estimated conservatively too: the reference per side is the 5-minute and 1-hour average weighted by how much actually traded in each, and the final estimate takes the worse of that and the last real trade.
