How the ranking thinks — five reasons a quoted margin isn't profit

1. A slot is time, not a container

Free-to-play has three GE slots, members eight. The binding constraint is not gold, it is slots — and a slot is occupied for however long the flip takes, not for a fixed window.

A flip that clears in twelve minutes for 5,000 gp earns 25,000 gp per slot-hour; one that ties the slot up for four hours to make 40,000 earns 10,000. So fill time is modelled, not assumed: traded volume gives an arrival rate, your undercut room gives the share of it you can capture, and the two give an expected round trip for both legs. That is the denominator of the ranking.

2. You have to get to the front of the queue

The Grand Exchange matches offers on price first, then on age: at the same price, an offer placed days ago has near-absolute priority over yours. So there are two ways to get filled — outbid the queue, or wait in it.

That is why air runes are a trap. Quoted at 5 buy / 6 sell, the 1 gp margin looks like a 20% return on 50,000 units. But to jump the queue you would have to buy at 6 and sell at 5, which loses money. You have zero room to compete, so you are stuck behind thousands of offers and bots for a margin of one coin. Every item gets an undercut room number: how much price improvement it can afford per side. Air runes have zero. That number is not a penalty of its own — it feeds the fill rate, because how far ahead of the queue you can buy your way is what determines how fast you fill. Those flips are not hidden; they fill slowly, and the ranking says so.

3. Your offer fills when you least want it to

A resting buy offer fills fastest exactly when the price is falling — someone is dumping into it — and then your sell leg is stranded above the market. Passive orders are selected against. Two readings of that hazard are scored: order-flow imbalance, which says who is being aggressive right now, and drift between the 5-minute and 1-hour averages, which says where the price has been going. Both are penalised only in the direction that hurts someone holding inventory between the legs.

4. The last print is not the market — and neither is a spike

A few hundred salmon dumped at 30 gp in the last minutes reads as "buy at 30" to every intraday number — while two weeks of sellers accepted 40. A big offer at 30 fills against that one dumper, then sits. And the mirror image is the manipulation trap: an item pumped above its normal level shows a juicy margin exactly while a merch clan is waiting to dump on whoever buys it.

So the leading candidates are re-checked against 14 days of history. What share of real volume traded at your prices is measured on detrended prices, so a dump stays visible whenever it happened but a steadily rising item is not punished for trading above last week — and that share feeds the fill rate, not the profit: a price only 5% of the market ever reached is not a flip earning 5% of its margin, it is a flip that takes twenty times as long.

Each item also gets its own mean-reversion fit rather than one rule for the whole game. "Above its two-week median" is a warning on a rune and the permanent condition of a raid unique, whose supply is fixed while demand grows. Where reversion is real the expected return over the actual holding period is charged or credited; where it is not, only a small capped trend term applies, because a rise and a merch-clan pump look identical in price data. Where the price level shifted mid-history — usually an update re-pricing the item — the fit spans two different markets and is not trusted at all.

5. The list itself is the biggest source of error

Ranking noisy estimates does not surface the best items. It surfaces the items whose estimation error happened to be largest and positive, and that is worst exactly where the data is thinnest. Every score is therefore pulled toward the market-wide average by an amount set by how much volume it rests on. The table shows both numbers: Measured before, EV/slot/h after. A wide gap means the measured number was mostly thin data. When no difference between the day's scores survives the noise, the tool says so instead of ranking anyway.

Intraday prices are estimated conservatively too: the reference per side is the 5-minute and 1-hour average weighted by how much actually traded in each, and the final estimate takes the worse of that and the last real trade. Around 57 items pay no GE tax at all, and the high-alchemy value puts a hard floor under the sell leg — both are priced in.
